# IAS machine family, 1946–1960 (roadmap #8)

Twenty-three nodes and twenty-two links: the computers built from, or closely modelled on, the Institute for Advanced Study machine designed under John von Neumann at Princeton. Its design reports were circulated freely, and most copies descend from the reports rather than the finished machine.

## Sources

- Wikipedia, "IAS machine" (list of IAS-derived machines), retrieved 22 September 2026.
- Wikipedia, "ILLIAC I", "BESK" and "JOHNNIAC", retrieved 22 September 2026.
- Burks, Goldstine and von Neumann, *Preliminary Discussion of the Logical Design of an Electronic Computing Instrument* (1946), as the root node.

Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA 4.0. This dataset is a compilation of dates and places, not a copy of that text.

## Choices and caveats

- **Years** are first-operational years, rounded. Several machines ran partially a year before formal completion.
- **Places** are the site where each machine first ran. ORDVAC was built in Illinois but is placed at Aberdeen, where it was delivered.
- **The root** is the 1946 design reports, not the IAS machine itself (1952), since ORDVAC ran first.
- **BESM** appears in some IAS-family lists but was an independent design by S. A. Lebedev's group. It is included and marked `"inferred": true`.
- **SEAC** appears in some lists but followed the EDVAC design. It is left out.
- **Second-generation links** (for example, ILLIAC → SILLIAC, MISTIC, CYCLONE and BESK → SMIL, FACIT EDB, SARA, DASK) follow the machines' own histories as given in the sources.

Corrections are welcome. The file is plain JSON and the viewer reloads it as is.
